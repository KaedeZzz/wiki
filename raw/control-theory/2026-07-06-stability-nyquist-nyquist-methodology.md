[[Nyquist Diagram]]

1. Draw a pole-zero diagram for open loop system $G(z)$, i.e. denote all positions of poles and zeros on a complex plane.
2. Count poles strictly outside unit circle: this is the number $\mathcal C$ of encirclements of $-\frac{1}{K}$necessary to achieve stability.
3. Draw the Nyquist locus for $\theta$ from $0$ to $\pi$.
4. Work out values of $G(0)$ and $G(j\pi)$ and any values of spectacular interest. Denote the direction of the locus.
5. Complete the graph for $\theta$ from $\pi$ to $2\pi$ according to conjugate symmetry property of the Nyquist diagram.
6. If the open loop system has poles on unit circle, work out asymptotes as the locus goes to infinity.
7. Apply the [[Nyquist Stability Criterion]] to determine range(s) of $K$ for which closed loop system is stable.

Always assist with unit circle diagram!
Check: closed-loop stability has poles within the unit circle.
